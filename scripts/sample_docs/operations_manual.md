Muara Logistics Sdn Bhd - Operations, Compliance and Sustainability Manual (2026 edition)

FICTIONAL SAMPLE DOCUMENT. Muara Logistics is a fictional company created for the Freight Ops Copilot portfolio project. Rules below are simplified examples written for the demo; always verify regulatory requirements with the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM), the relevant ministries and the port operators before acting on them.

Section 1. Company Profile and Services

1.1 Muara Logistics is a third-party logistics (3PL) provider based in Pulau Indah, Port Klang, Selangor. We provide ground handling for international freight forwarders: customs clearance, container haulage from Westport and Northport, warehousing and final delivery anywhere in Peninsular Malaysia, plus coastal shipping coordination to Sabah and Sarawak.

1.2 Our core commodities are chemicals (including dangerous goods), palm oil and palm-based products, and plastics (polymer resins and finished plastic goods). General cargo is also accepted.

1.3 We operate two warehouses: a general cargo warehouse of 4,000 pallet positions in Pulau Indah and a licensed dangerous-goods (DG) store of 800 pallet positions with segregated bays and bunded floors.

Section 2. Customs Clearance

2.1 Import shipments are declared on Customs Form K1 and export shipments on Customs Form K2, submitted electronically through the uCustoms system. Transshipment and movement of goods between customs areas use Form K8.

2.2 A customs declaration must be supported by the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, and any import or export permits required for the goods. Missing permits are the most common cause of clearance delays.

2.3 The clearance team must submit the declaration no later than 24 hours after the vessel discharge schedule is confirmed. Shipments selected for physical inspection must be moved to the inspection bay within the same working day.

2.4 Duties and sales tax are paid by the importer of record. Muara Logistics may advance duty payments only with written approval from the Finance Manager and a signed indemnity from the forwarder.

Section 3. Dangerous Goods - Chemicals

3.1 Every chemical shipment requires a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) no older than five years and a dangerous goods declaration stating the UN number, proper shipping name, hazard class and packing group.

3.2 Containers carrying dangerous goods must display the correct hazard placards on all four sides. Drivers carrying DG must hold a valid GDL (goods driving licence) with DG endorsement and carry the SDS and emergency response information in the cab.

3.3 Segregation rules in the DG store: Class 3 flammable liquids must be stored at least 3 metres away from Class 5.1 oxidisers; Class 8 corrosives must be stored on bunded floors; Class 2 gas cylinders must be stored upright and chained. Incompatible classes must never share a bay.

3.4 Westport and Northport limit how long dangerous goods may stay in the terminal. Our rule: DG containers are collected within 24 hours of discharge and never parked at our yard overnight unless inside the licensed DG area.

3.5 Chemical jobs carry a dangerous-goods surcharge of 25 percent on haulage and a DG documentation fee per container.

3.6 Any spill or leak must be reported immediately to the Operations Manager and the Department of Environment hotline. Use the spill kits located at every DG bay and evacuate if vapour is detected.

Section 4. Palm Oil and Palm-Based Products

4.1 Crude palm oil (CPO) and refined palm products move in flexitanks inside 20ft containers or in ISO tank containers. Flexitanks must be fitted by certified installers and inspected for leaks before loading.

4.2 CPO solidifies at low temperatures. ISO tanks and flexitanks with heating pads must be heated to between 50 and 55 degrees Celsius before discharge; never exceed 60 degrees Celsius because it damages product quality.

4.3 Export consignments must include the MSPO (Malaysian Sustainable Palm Oil) certificate and, where the buyer requires it, the MPOB licence number of the exporter. Shipments to the European Union also require due-diligence and traceability documents under the EU Deforestation Regulation.

4.4 Palm oil volumes peak during the harvest season from September to November. Planners must secure extra prime movers and flexitank stock by August each year.

4.5 Palm oil jobs carry a 10 percent haulage surcharge and a tank inspection and heating fee per container.

Section 5. Plastics

5.1 Plastic resins (polyethylene, polypropylene, PVC) are shipped in 25 kg bags on pallets or in 1 tonne jumbo bags. Pallets must be shrink-wrapped and kept dry; resin contaminated by moisture is rejected by manufacturers.

5.2 Importing plastic scrap or plastic waste into Malaysia is tightly restricted and requires approval and permits from the relevant authorities. Muara Logistics does not handle plastic waste or scrap shipments under any circumstances.

5.3 Year-end is the peak season for plastics as manufacturers build stock before Chinese New Year.

Section 6. Haulage Operations

6.1 Haulage is performed by prime movers with 20ft or 40ft trailers. The verified gross mass of a container on our trailers must not exceed 25,000 kg, in line with the Association of Malaysian Hauliers limit at Port Klang. Overweight containers must be re-stuffed or moved with a special permit.

6.2 Drivers must complete a pre-trip inspection covering tyres, brakes, lights, twist locks and the container seal number before leaving the port.

6.3 Rail (KTMB intermodal) is offered for Ipoh and Prai (Penang). For distances above 200 km, planners must quote both truck and rail options so the customer can compare cost and emissions.

6.4 Shipments to Sabah and Sarawak travel by coastal shipping from Port Klang. Delivery inside East Malaysia is subcontracted to partner hauliers and quoted separately.

Section 7. Demurrage, Detention and Storage

7.1 Demurrage is charged by the shipping line when a full container stays in the port beyond the free time. Detention is charged when the customer keeps the container outside the port beyond the free days before returning it empty.

7.2 Typical free time at Port Klang is 3 days for port storage and 5 to 7 days for container detention, depending on the shipping line. Always confirm the free time on the delivery order.

7.3 The planning team reviews all containers daily. Any container within one day of its free time expiring must be escalated to the Operations Manager and prioritised for collection or return.

Section 8. Warehousing

8.1 Storage is charged per pallet per day, and handling is charged per pallet for receiving and dispatch. Dangerous goods stored in the DG warehouse are charged at 1.8 times the general storage rate.

8.2 Stock must be managed first-in-first-out (FIFO), except palm-based products with batch expiry dates, which use first-expired-first-out (FEFO).

8.3 Cycle counts are carried out weekly for DG stock and monthly for general cargo. Inventory variance above 0.5 percent must be investigated within 48 hours.

Section 9. Quotations and Pricing

9.1 Quotations are built from the company rate card: diesel (updated weekly from the Ministry of Finance retail price), truck maintenance per kilometre, fixed truck cost and driver time per hour, tolls, port handling charges, customs fees, cargo surcharges and warehousing.

9.2 The standard margin on cost is 15 percent. Discounts above 5 percent require approval from the Commercial Manager.

9.3 Quotes are valid for 7 days because diesel prices change weekly.

Section 10. Sustainability and ESG

10.1 Muara Logistics commits to reduce fleet emissions per TEU moved by 30 percent by 2030 against a 2024 baseline, through rail modal shift, route optimisation and newer Euro 5 prime movers.

10.2 Emissions are calculated from actual diesel consumption using 2.68 kg of CO2 per litre of diesel. Every quotation shows the estimated CO2e next to the price.

10.3 Idling is limited to 5 minutes. Drivers must switch off engines while waiting in port queues.

10.4 Subcontracted hauliers must report fuel use quarterly and comply with the company code of conduct, including fair working hours and no forced labour.
